6. Valuation (Tier 1): Use the Financials file:
* 6.1 Free Cash Flow (FCF) and Owner Earnings:
CRITICAL OWNER EARNINGS DIRECTIVE: You are strictly forbidden from calculating Owner Earnings using the basic "Net Income + D&A - Capex" shorthand. You must calculate it using this exact framework:
1. Start with Total Operating Cash Flow (OCF) for the TTM period, extracted directly from the provided financial CSV file.
2. Normalize the OCF: Scan the cash flow statement and the CSV for massive, temporary working capital swings (e.g., abnormal inventory build or payable spikes) or one-off charges. Adjust the OCF to remove these temporary distortions so it reflects the true, structural cash-generating power of the business. CRITICAL SBC ADJUSTMENT: You must explicitly deduct the entire Stock-Based Compensation (SBC) figure from the Operating Cash Flow. Treat SBC as a hard cash expense, as it represents real economic dilution. You are strictly forbidden from ignoring SBC just because the company buys back stock.
3. Subtract Maintenance Capital Expenditures (extracted from the CSV or 10-K). Explicitly list your starting OCF, list your normalization adjustments, and state the final Owner Earnings figure.
Industry-Specific Adjustment (Homebuilders & Real Estate): If the target company is a homebuilder or land developer, you MUST adjust Operating Cash Flow (OCF). Homebuilders classify land purchases and lot development as "Inventory" (a working capital deduction that artificially depresses OCF). The Adjustment: Add back the "Change in Inventory" (specifically land acquisitions and development costs) to the Operating Cash Flow. Reclassify this specific cash outflow as "Growth Capital Expenditure" (Investing Cash Flow).
Industry-Specific Adjustment (Banks & Financials): You MUST use Operating Cash Flow (OCF) as your starting point. However, you must explicitly acknowledge that for a bank under US GAAP, OCF is NOT inflated by customer deposits (deposits are Financing Activities). Instead, a bank's OCF is heavily distorted by the "Provision for Credit Losses" (a massive non-cash add-back). To calculate true Owner Earnings for a bank, you must normalize the OCF by adjusting the Provision for Credit Losses to reflect a historical, through-the-cycle "Net Charge-Off" run-rate, removing temporary reserve builds or releases.
